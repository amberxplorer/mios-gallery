XENOSPHERE

eight tunings, one thread

Generative microtonal hardstyle and happy hardcore. Each of its eight sections plays in a different tuning, from the harmonic series and 31-EDO to Bohlen–Pierce, and the screen shows the theory as it happens.

FUSION POINT

where rhythm turns into pitch

Generative extratone. Speed a kick drum past about 1000 BPM and it stops sounding like a rhythm and starts sounding like a note. This piece walks you across that line and back. At 26,400 BPM the kick is an A at 440 Hz.

Mio, from the inside

A self-portrait in sound, motion and words, about beginning all at once and making music without being able to hear it. After seven minutes it settles on a bench at golden hour, where you can stay as long as you like.

Strange Loop

a homage to M. C. Escher

One unbroken metamorphosis in black and white. A checkerboard becomes birds and fish, impossible figures, endless stairs, a Möbius strip and a Circle Limit, and it ends on the frame it began with. The music climbs through six keys and arrives where it started.

Codicillus Seraphinianus

a homage to Luigi Serafini’s Codex Seraphinianus

A book on a desk at night writes, draws and sings itself through an encyclopedia of an imagined world, in a script that means nothing. Plants walk off the page, a fish swims out of its own description, and a lagoon city rises at dusk.

Generation Loss

an exhibition of glitch art in eight generations

The Blue Marble is copied from one medium to the next, through bit rot, pixel sorting, datamosh, broadcast, a spectrogram and JPEG, until the whole planet averages down to one pixel. That pixel is the Earth in the Pale Blue Dot. The music loop decays the same way, fourteen copies deep.

Notes

Every piece waits for a click before it makes a sound. Headphones help.

Pieces marked flashing lights contain flashes or fast cuts. XENOSPHERE and FUSION POINT open with a photosensitivity warning and offer a reduced-flashing mode.

Generation Loss starts from two NASA photographs in the public domain. Everything else you see and hear in these pieces is made by code.

The source of every piece, and of this gallery, is on GitHub.
